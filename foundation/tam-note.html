Assumptions we are making

One load-bearing assumption
The whole TAM rests on the per-community entitlement filter (30–70%). Swing factor = the Portuguese (Luxembourg's largest community). Reasoned from STATEC first-generation + emigration-profile data; harden via STATEC age-at-migration by nationality.
Gate is work history, not age
Counts anyone with entitlements in 2+ systems, any age. The 35–60 band is a segmentation overlay (highest engagement), not a qualifying filter — the earlier version wrongly cut the market to it.
Nationals layer is softest
Sized from the naturalization gap and return-migration patterns; no clean per-origin naturalization data pulled yet.
Converges with the original estimate
All-ages ~140–200K lands near the original ~130–150K — but now entitlement-sourced and age-explicit, not hand-waved. Same magnitude, honest derivation.
Not counted: cross-border commuters / frontaliers (~231K — single pension system, no multi-country problem); second-generation residents who never worked abroad; residents who worked only in Luxembourg (one entitlement).

Assumptions & honesty notes

One load-bearing assumption
The whole TAM rests on the 50–70% entitlement filter. It's a sourced range (work-motivation + age-at-migration), not invented — but it's the number to attack. Harden it by pulling Eurostat migr_imm1ctz age-at-arrival bands for an exact "25+" share.
Anchor came in lower than expected
We'd assumed ~17M; the clean EU-27 figure is 14.1M (the 17M was the older EU-28 count, including the UK). Using the verified number, not the remembered one.
Materiality not filtered
"2+ entitlements" includes short or small second stints that may never vest meaningfully — so 7–10M is generous as a demand figure; a materiality haircut lowers it.
Undercount, opposite direction
Return-migrants (worked abroad, came home) have the problem but aren't in a "living abroad" stock — pushing the true figure up. Roughly offsets the materiality haircut.

Sources

EC — Annual Report on Intra-EU Labour Mobility 2024 — 10.1M working-age EU/EFTA citizens living + working in another member state (ref. 2022/23).

Eurostat — 1.9M EU/EFTA aged 65+ living outside country of citizenship (2018); ~17M citizens living in another member state (gross ceiling).

HIVA-KU Leuven for the European Commission — "Cross-border pensions, Reference Year 2024": 6.4M cross-border pensions / €31bn paid (payment flows); ~1.48M people drawing pensions from 2+ member states; 3.2% of EU pensioners draw from 2+ states (13% in Luxembourg).

EU Employment & Social Affairs Council (2024) — ~6 million people have worked in more than one EU country, cited in the 14-state push for faster cross-border pensions (reported via Europe Street News). A politically-cited figure, used as corroboration.

Portuguese-origin community estimate — STATEC / community sources: ~142K including naturalized vs 88.3K foreign-national.
