# Prevista — T3 Tax / Contribution Calendar Channel Frame (working draft v1)

*2026-04-27. Companion to T1 (relocation agencies) and T4 (EU institutions) channel work. T3 = "Annual tax / contribution calendar" — #3 in the first-1000 ranking, Q4-deadline-gated.*

## 0. Geographic scope (added later same session)

**For LU launch, T3 is a content/SEO play, not a partnership channel.** The VIAC analysis below applies when Prevista expands to CH; at LU launch the digital-distributor partner doesn't exist. See § 10.

Reason in one line: LU's Art. 111bis market is **insurance-led** (Foyer, Lalux, La Mondiale, AXA Lux), there is no LU equivalent of VIAC/finpension/frankly., and the deduction value per user (~€1,280/yr) is roughly half of CH's (~CHF 2,200/yr) — too small to justify the operational complexity of an insurance-distributor partnership at this stage.

**Sections 1–9 below = the CH-expansion playbook, stays valid for that geography.** Section 10 = the LU-launch alternative.

## 1. The trigger and its calendar

T3 is the only trigger with a hard external deadline. Every year, Profile A users hit at least one of:

| Jurisdiction | Product | Cap (2026) | Deadline | Surge window |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CH | Pillar 3a (employed) | CHF 7,258/yr | Dec 31 (money cleared) | Oct–Dec |
| CH | Pillar 3a (self-employed) | up to CHF 36,288/yr | Dec 31 | Oct–Dec |
| FR | PER | ~10% of income, capped ~€35K | Dec 31 (tax year) | Nov–Dec |
| LU | Art. 111bis | €3,200/yr | Dec 31 | Nov–Dec |
| FR/DE/CH/LU | Tax filing | varies (Apr–Jul) | filing season | Q1–Q2 |

**Implication for partner timing:** to catch Q4 2026, partner conversations must start *now* (April–May) and conclude by August. Slip past Aug = wait 12 months for the next surge.

## 2. T3 differs structurally from T1 and T4

For both T1 (relocation agencies) and T4 (EU institutions), the partner gets non-cash value (service-line differentiation, slot-filling) and money does not change hands.

T3 partners are **product distributors whose revenue depends on user contributions**. The clean value exchange is direct revenue alignment — and an affiliate/referral fee is normal in this industry, not a brand violation, *if* disclosed transparently in the user UI ("Prevista receives a commission if you open a 3a account through us — this helps fund the service"). This unlocks a fundamentally different commercial model for T3 than T1/T4.

## 3. Partner types — value-exchange tests

### A. Digital Pillar 3a / PER providers (primary)

| Pain we solve | Value they send |
|---|---|
| Annual CAC pressure during Q4 surge; need to convert intent into signups in 8–10 weeks | Distribution to existing customer base (email, in-app); brand legitimacy; affiliate fee per converted signup or contribution |

**Competitive overlap:** zero. We orient and recommend; they distribute regulated savings products. Different value chain.

**Sub-segments:**
- **CH:** VIAC (CHF 5B AUM), finpension (60K customers, CHF 5B AUM), frankly. (ZKB-backed, more conservative culture).
- **FR:** Yomoni (digital, marketing-led), Nalo, Goodvest (ESG), Boursorama Banque (mass-market).
- **LU:** Foyer, Lalux dominate Art. 111bis (insurance-led, slower decisions).

### B. Cross-border tax advisors (secondary, year-round)

| Pain we solve | Value they send |
|---|---|
| 3–6 unbillable advisor hours/client during filing season collecting fragmented data | Referrals when clients ask "should I contribute to PER/3a/111bis?" — they get this question constantly |

**Competitive overlap:** minimal — we don't do tax filing, they don't do orientation. **Boutiques that sell pension-and-tax advice together have some overlap; Big 4 GMS practices don't.**

### C. Banks with Pillar 3a / PER desks (slower, larger)

| Pain we solve | Value they send |
|---|---|
| Customer engagement on a low-touch product; retention | Mass-scale distribution; brand halo |

**Competitive overlap:** banks have wealth-management arms that may overlap with Prevista's roadmap if we drift toward advice. Orientation-only Prevista is fine.

**Honest weakness:** procurement is slow (12–18 months). Not the fast first-contact.

### D. Tax-prep software / online savings platforms

Smaller niche, less Profile-A density. Skip for v1.

## 5. First-contact target — VIAC

**Why VIAC over finpension or frankly.:**
- **Founder-led, partnership-evident:** founders Daniel Peter, Jonas Gusset, Christian Mathis still active. VIAC has an existing structural partnership with Bank WIR (mortgages + custody) — partnership decisions get made.
- **Digital-native culture:** VIAC ships and decides quickly. finpension is comparable in scale but has a more institutional culture; frankly. is ZKB-owned (slowest).
- **Better English UX:** Profile A is multinational. VIAC is the only one of the three with consistent English-first UX, which matters for international users in CH.
- **Profile-A density:** VIAC's user base skews young-mid professional, technology-comfortable, internationally-mobile — exactly Profile A.
- **CH Pillar 3a is the highest-value annual deduction in our addressable market** (CHF 7,258/yr deductible, ~50% of eligible CH residents don't contribute despite the deduction = huge education gap that Prevista closes).

## 6. What we offer VIAC

A **Q4 deadline campaign partnership**, live mid-September through end-December.

**Deliverables:**
1. **Co-branded landing page** — `prevista.com/3a/viac` and reciprocal at `viac.ch/prevista`. Three-variable template same as the relocation-agency template; reuses existing infra.
2. **Educational content cross-promotion** — VIAC publishes an article "How to know if you actually need a Pillar 3a" linking to Prevista's free gap calculator. Prevista publishes "Maximising your CHF 7,258 — a 5-minute walkthrough" linking to VIAC.
3. **Q4 deadline reminder co-marketing** — joint email push in early November to both lists with the deadline countdown and a deduction calculator.
4. **In-product VIAC recommendation** — Prevista users whose gap analysis recommends a CH Pillar 3a contribution see VIAC as the suggested provider in their action list, with a transparent disclosure ("Prevista receives a commission — this helps fund the service") and a comparison link to alternatives.

**What this is NOT:**
- Not exclusivity — Prevista will recommend competitor 3a providers where they fit better (e.g., finpension for sophisticated investors, frankly. for ZKB clients). Disclosure-led, not exclusive.
- Not regulated advice — orientation only.
- Not a fixed marketing fee — performance-based affiliate only.

## 8. First-contact mechanic

**Target:** Daniel Peter (CEO/co-founder) or Christian Mathis. LinkedIn + email. Cold email is fine — VIAC's CEO has historically been accessible to fintech founders.

## 10. T3 in LU launch — content / SEO play (not partnership)

The LU 111bis market is too thin and too insurance-led for a digital partnership. The right T3 motion at LU launch is:

| Asset | Detail | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **111bis deadline calculator** | "Should you contribute to Art. 111bis before Dec 31?" — takes user income, marginal rate, age; outputs €X tax saved this year if contribute. Lives at `prevista.com/lu/111bis-calculator` | 1–2 dev days |
| **Q4 SEO content sprint** | 4–6 articles covering 111bis mechanics, LU vs FR PER comparison for cross-border commuters, common mistakes (no contribution proof, late wire). Published Sept–Oct. | 4–6 days writing + SEO |
| **Email reminder to existing Prevista users** | Two-touch sequence (early Nov + early Dec) gated to LU-resident users in Prevista's user base | 1 day |
| **Insurer-distributor outreach (defer)** | Foyer, Lalux, La Mondiale Partenaire — slow, traditional, single-shot meetings only. **Defer to year 2** unless one of them inbounds first. | 0 days at launch |

**Realistic LU-launch contribution from T3 content/SEO: ~30–80 paid users in year 1.** Smaller than the original CH-VIAC estimate (60–150) because we're capturing native intent only (no partner distribution amplification).

**When to revive partnership angle:**
- When Prevista expands to CH → execute § 5 (VIAC first contact) on the CH expansion timeline.
- If a LU insurer (likely Foyer or Lalux) inbounds asking about cross-marketing → opportunistic, not pursuit.
