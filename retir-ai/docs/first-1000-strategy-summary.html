Prevista · Go-to-market vision

How we reach the first 1,000 paying customers.

A Luxembourg beachhead. Five pains. Three sequenced phases. No outside capital.

01Who we build for

Internationally-mobile European professionals — aged 45 to 60, with pension entitlements split across two or three EU/EFTA jurisdictions. They have €100K–€500K of pension wealth they can't see in one place, and five to twenty years left to do something about it.

We don't build for retirees. We build for the years in which the gap can still be closed — late enough that the stakes feel real, early enough that contributions, retirement age, and residency choices still move the number.

Mats Karlsson is the median user: 45, Luxembourg resident, careers in France, Switzerland, and Luxembourg, target retirement income €5,500/month, current verified projection €3,840/month. A €498,000 shortfall over a 25-year drawdown — and twenty years to fix it.

02The five moments they become reachable

Profile A users don't sit in passive search mode. They become acquirable at five distinct life moments — each carrying its own pain, its own behavioural signal, and its own channel.

  • "I'm moving to a new country in three months and nobody told me about pension transfer."

    T1 — Cross-border move in-flight · reached through relocation agencies, employer HR, expat communities

  • "I'm 58. Where do I retire? When do I claim across three pension systems?"

    T4 — Approaching retirement while mobile · reached through EU institutions, pre-retirement seminars, financial advisors

  • "I left Switzerland four years ago and never collected my workplace pension."

    T2 — Leaving a country, with money left behind · reached through PR, SEO, viral mini-tools

  • "It's October. I have a tax-deduction window I'll lose if I don't act."

    T3 — Annual contribution calendar · reached through Q4 SEO sprints, tax-advisor partnerships

  • "I want to use my Swiss 3a or French PER for property abroad — what are the rules?"

    T5 — Cross-border property purchase · reached through notary partnerships

03Three motions to reach them

The five pains map to three ways of reaching people. Each plays a different role — and each runs on a different rhythm.

Motion Pains served Role
Content & SEO T2 leaving · T3 tax calendar · T5 property The engine — reaches people in self-serve mode, on a deadline, or searching for a specific answer. Carries Year 1 volume. Steady, daily.
Partnerships T1 move · T4 retirement The moat — places us inside trusted institutions at the moment of pain. Compounds in Year 2 and beyond. Recurring, quarterly.
Earned media T2 leaving (primary) · halo across all five The catalyst — concentrated bursts of attention from PR hooks (e.g. "CHF 6.7B unclaimed Swiss pensions"). Episodic, opportunistic.

Content is what a small team can ship alone. Partnerships need counterparties. Earned media needs a story worth telling. We design the plan around those asymmetries: content is what we control, partnerships are what we earn, earned media is what we catalyse when something genuinely newsworthy is on the table.

Why these partners actually want us

Partnerships fail when one side is doing the other a favour. Both of our launch-phase partner types have a concrete commercial or institutional reason to put us in front of their people — without us asking them to.

Partner type · T1

Relocation agencies (LU)

Their corporate clients — multinationals sending staff to Luxembourg — are increasingly asking "and what about pensions?" The agencies' current answer is "ask a tax advisor." That's a gap in their offering at exactly the moment they're competing for or renewing corporate contracts worth €100K–€1M per year.

We let them turn pension orientation into a service line they can market. They get a co-branded landing page, drop-in welcome-pack copy, a quarterly cross-border-pensions workshop, and three months of free Pro for every assignee. It costs them nothing to deliver, and it visibly strengthens their commercial story.

What we ask in return: a referral link in their day-1 onboarding email. No data exchange. No regulated advice. No procurement.

Partner type · T4

EU institutions (EIB first)

EIB staff are Profile A by definition — internationally recruited, multi-jurisdiction careers, high salaries. Cross-border pension complexity is higher at EIB than in the private sector: staff carry the EIB occupational scheme on top of prior national rights from France, Germany, Italy, Spain. Multi-pillar claim-timing is a real, unsolved question that the institution itself doesn't have a satisfying answer to.

The College of Staff Representatives at EIB Luxembourg has an explicit mandate to support active and retired staff. A 60-minute pre-retirement workshop on the cross-border pensions question fills a documented gap in their pre-retirement programme. No money flows. No commercial conflict. They get genuinely useful content for their people; we get a credible institutional reference.

The halo: "Delivered at the EIB" opens the European Commission, the ECJ, and the Court of Auditors — all Luxembourg-anchored, all carrying the same Profile-A staff demographic.

Both relationships sit on the same principle: we are not asking the partner to send us their customers. We are giving the partner a better answer to a question their customers are already asking. The referral flow is a side-effect of solving their problem.

How the partnership network scales with country expansion

The country engines compound — every new engine completes the picture for prior users in that corridor. Institutional partnerships compound the same way — but only the right kind. We sort them into three tiers based on whether the relationship buys us only Luxembourg, or buys us a template that scales across the country roadmap.

Tier A · templated

One relationship → many countries

AMCHAM Luxembourg — sister chambers in DE, FR, BE, NL, CH, IT, ES, AT, PT, UK. Landing AMCHAM LU as a member-benefit partner opens a templated motion across 10 of our first 13 country engines.

The Local network — English-language expat news with editions in FR, DE, CH, AT, ES, IT, SE, DK. Eight editions, almost exactly matching our M1–M36 country roadmap. One editorial relationship strategy, eight country-launch PR moments.

Embassy / consular networks — every country hosts every other EU country's consulates. Templated motion: same outreach in DE in M13 as in LU at launch.

Compounding pattern: single relationship investment, payback across 5–10 countries.

Tier B · corridor-anchored

Compound at specific engine launches

Bilateral chambers — French-LU (FR engine live M1, full value from day one) · Portuguese-LU (PT engine M3) · German-LU (DE engine M13, value doubles when bilateral data flow opens) · Belgian-LU (BE engine M16).

EU institutions — EIB / Commission / ECJ / Court of Auditors all Luxembourg-anchored today. As corridor maturity grows, the same template extends to Brussels (Commission HQ), Strasbourg (Parliament), Frankfurt (ECB).

Compounding pattern: each relationship is a paired bet with a specific corridor activation. Some pay back immediately; others are 12-month investments that compound at engine launch.

Tier C · LU-only

One-shot, no template

Delano · Paperjam · Luxembourg Times · RTL Today · Luxemburger Wort — local press. Useful for LU launch PR, but each country has its own English-language and local-language outlets to pitch separately.

Chambre des salariés · ADEM — LU-specific labour and employment institutions. Each country has different architecture (Betriebsrat in DE, CGT in FR, Travail.Suisse in CH); not portable.

Compounding pattern: none. Pursue opportunistically, not as priority. Every hour spent here is an hour not spent on a Tier A or B relationship.

The strategic implication: pre-launch institutional outreach is selected for compounding alignment with the country roadmap, not for Year-1 Luxembourg ramp alone. AMCHAM and The Local Network in particular are template investments — landing them buys us the next eight engine launches at half the relationship cost.

04Three phases, sequenced by evidence

We don't try to launch everything at once. The plan moves in three phases — each only starts when the previous one has produced its evidence.

Phase 0 · M−3 → M0

Validate demand

A free public pension calculator goes live three months before the platform. The only call-to-action is "Join the wishlist." Founder labour, zero spend, organic SEO and community seeding only. The point is to prove people care enough to give us their email before the product even exists.

Target: 150–450 wishlist signups by launch. Floor of 150 is the validation threshold; the upper end implies a strong PR or community amplification moment.

Phase 1 · M1 → M6

Launch & activate

Platform goes live. The wishlist gets a "we're live" email — the warmest cohort we'll ever have. Content engine starts. First partnership pilots seeded (relocation agencies, EU institutions). Founder-funded; no external capital. The job here is to measure conversion, not scale it.

Outcome: validated funnel rates; first paying subscribers.

Phase 2 · M7 → M18

Compound to break-even

Partnership channel deals start producing flow. Content compounds month-on-month. Lead-gen revenue (referred capital to partner platforms) turns on. Still no paid acquisition. Cumulative break-even at month 18 — funded entirely from revenue and founder capital.

Milestone: first 1,000 paying customers, cumulative cash positive.

Each phase only starts when the previous phase has produced its evidence. By launch we know whether anyone wants this. By month six we know whether they pay. By month eighteen we know whether the business funds itself.

05The path to a thousand — quarter by quarter

"First 1,000 customers" means paying subscribers at €4.99/month. In the base case the model crosses 1,000 paid at month 17; in the upside case at month 13. Cumulative cash break-even lands one month later, at M18. That's the destination — but the path matters more than the line, so here's how it actually accumulates.

The shape of the curve

The first 100 paying customers take six times longer to reach than the next 900. This is not a flaw — it's the compounding signature of a content-led, partnership-seeded funnel. SEO pages need 4–6 months to index and rank. Partnerships have 5–9 month sales cycles. Once both motions are in flow, the curve steepens fast.

The funnel underneath — paying is ~12% of signups

A note on freemium. The 1,349 paying customers at M18 sit underneath a much larger funnel — most users never pay, and that's by design. Free users generate referral revenue (the lead-gen stream — when they consolidate pensions through one of our partner platforms, we earn a small annual fee on the referred capital), they build the data corpus that makes the engines smarter, and they tell us which countries to launch next.

Where the 1,000 actually come from

By the time we cross the milestone at M17, the conservative model split looks like this:

Channel Pains served Share of first 1,000
Content / SEO · organic search, viral mini-tools T2 leaving · T3 tax calendar · T5 property ~94%
Partnerships · agency referrals, EU institution workshops T1 move · T4 retirement ~6%
Paid acquisition — 0%

Two things to read into this. First, partnership share is structurally small in the first 1,000 because partnership cycles are slow and the first deals only start producing flow at M7 — but that share triples by Year 3 as the agency network matures and EU-institution workshops become a recurring quarterly fixture.

Second, the 6% figure reflects a deliberately narrow definition: only the steady-state agency-style partnership channel is counted. Templated institutional outreach (AMCHAM, bilateral chambers) and earned-media catalysts (newspaper hits, viral PR) are absorbed into the "content" bucket via attribution, or not modelled at all. If those channels land at the rates we'd expect in Luxembourg, partnership-attributed share of the first 1,000 plausibly sits closer to 15–25% — with the rest of the lift coming through templated relationships that we'll claim back in Years 2–3 as country engines activate.

The honest read: the named-partnership work happening now is how we earn channel quality that compounds — not the lever that gets us to a thousand. Content carries Year 1; partnerships are the moat that makes Year 3 defensible.

06Why this works

It's grounded in a model, not a hope

Every number above comes from a financial model with explicit assumptions: 4% organic reach of addressable corridor in Year 1, 12% paid conversion at €4.99, 3% monthly churn declining as cohorts mature, partnership ramp from one active partner at month seven to three by month twelve. The model reaches cumulative break-even at month 18 with zero paid acquisition and zero outside capital.

It respects what a small team can actually do

Two co-founders. Content production and SEO are the only Year 1 channels that don't require counterparty velocity. Partnerships are seeded in Year 1 and compound in Year 2 — no Year 1 milestone depends on a partner signing fast. The plan is robust to two of the three motions under-performing.

The named partnerships are the moat, not the engine

LuxRelo, ERS, MMC, the EIB pre-retirement office, VIAC, Spuerkeess — the work to seed these is real and ongoing. But we don't bet Year 1 on them landing fast. They are positioned as the long-term channel quality that becomes uncatchable in Year 3, not the volume that gets us to the first thousand.

Institutional partnerships compound the same way country engines do

The country roadmap compounds: every new engine completes the picture for prior users. The institutional partnership network compounds in the same shape — but only the right kind. Tier-A relationships (AMCHAM, The Local network, embassy networks) and Tier-B relationships (bilateral chambers, EU institutions) are deliberately chosen because they template across the country roadmap. A single AMCHAM Luxembourg partnership opens warm-intro paths to ten national AmChams as those engines activate. One editorial relationship with The Local Network produces a country-launch PR moment in eight different countries. The same compounding spine that drives the country expansion also governs how the partnership network builds.

Lead-gen is the asset

Every signup, paying or not, becomes part of a referred-capital pool that compounds at roughly 9% per year. By Year 5 this stream is a fifth of total revenue. By Year 10 it's the majority. We don't manage assets — we route them, and the routing fee compounds. The freemium tier exists to fuel that compounding.

Vision in one line:
Meet five pains in three motions, sequence each phase on evidence, choose every partnership for how it compounds with the country map — and let a freemium funnel feed a compounding asset underneath a subscription that pays the lights.
